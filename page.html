What do you need to know about the Earth?

Describe your need in your own words, in any language. The navigator places it in the EARSC taxonomy of Earth observation services, lists the matching layers of the DestinE Data Lake with a confidence level, then tells you how to get the data and how to process them. It can also point to existing e-shape pilot services and judge their fitness for your need.

From your need to the data: the Data-Information-Value view

This is the Data-Information-Value framework of the DestinE co-design toolkit (template 2.0.1), filled in from your request and read from left to right: the value you seek depends on information, which depends on data, which depend on an infrastructure. Select a box to see what it depends on.

Prototypes on demand: from data to information, one ingredient at a time

Select an ingredient, here or in the right-hand lane of “Valuable information” in the graph. For a known recipe, the page prepares the plan of a prototype for your area and period, and the local app generates it. For any other ingredient, Claude can draft a method. In both cases every step states its nature: documented method, choice, or interpretation that has not been validated.

The data to assemble

One line per ingredient and dataset: what is in the DestinE Data Lake, what exists elsewhere, what is missing. It summarises the analysis above, so it is a proposal to check, not a validated specification.

Reword the view in your own words

The two left columns, the expected information and its ingredients are yours. Correct them and the tool places your need again and recomputes the data and infrastructure columns. If a text contains several distinct elements, put one per line: each becomes its own box. The information is a proposal by Claude; the EARSC category under it only classifies it.

Co-design workshops to ask for, by level of uncertainty

The view above shows what is known; this list addresses what is not. It follows the DestinE co-design toolkit, which uses the framework to reveal unknowns and to choose workshops accordingly. The workshops are run with a service provider: this tells you what to ask for, in which order, and what to bring.

Existing services you could reuse: e-shape pilots

Taxonomy: EARSC portal. Sensor to variable ratings, sensor resolution and revisit: WMO OSCAR. The resolution of a collection is read from the catalogue text when it states one, otherwise it is the sensor's best case on its OSCAR page; nothing is filled from memory, and many collections state none. The tables that link variables, instruments and product families to services are proposals that have not been validated; treat every layer as a lead to check. A layer is “ready-to-use” when the collection carries the information itself, and “needs processing” when it is one input among others. Processing resources are listed only where a link was verified; other services show none. Page built on .

Data-Information-Value framework: DestinE co-design toolkit, worksheet 2.0 and template 2.0.1 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.20793534, CC BY 4.0). Workshop recommendations paraphrase worksheets 2.0, 3.1 to 3.4, 4.0 and 4.1 of the same toolkit; the uncertainty levels are computed by simple rules from the confidence scores and gaps of the view.

e-shape pilots: webservice-energy catalogue records (snapshot of ) and the project's compiled Data Management Plans (self-declared, sprints 2021–2023). Compliance levels are those targeted for the end of sprint 2, not a verified current status. Contacts may have changed. Input data and infrastructure are detected by keyword in the pilots' own texts; the link from a pilot to an EARSC service was assigned by hand and is not validated. The fit, “why” and “to check” texts are Claude's reading; compliance, adaptation flags and contacts come from the source files.
